Moving from regular matchmaking to FACEIT does not replace the game. You still play Counter-Strike 2, Valorant, Overwatch 2, or Siege. What changes is the competitive wrapper: who hosts the match, how you are ranked, and which client rules apply.

That is the whole comparison. FACEIT is not a second version of CS2. Regular matchmaking is not “the FACEIT-free edition” of the same product page.

## What stays the same

The title, the maps, and the role you already know stay the same. A FACEIT CS2 queue is still CS2. A FACEIT Valorant lobby is still Valorant. If a listing on this site mentions a game, it is talking about that game inside FACEIT — not a reskinned standalone title.

The [Valorant listing](/valorant/) also works for regular Valorant, not only FACEIT queues.

## What usually changes

Three things show up in almost every “FACEIT vs matchmaking” search:

1. **The client and account path.** FACEIT has its own application and ranking. Publisher matchmaking stays inside the game.
2. **The ladder.** Premier, unrated, role-queue, or ranked Siege are publisher systems. FACEIT keeps a separate competitive identity.
3. **The platform environment.** FACEIT runs its own competitive rules and anti-cheat stack. This article does not document how that stack works or how anyone would evade it.

None of those changes invent a new shooter. They change where the match is hosted.

## Why this site still splits by game

Because the wrapper is shared and the products are not. CS2 modules, Valorant modules, Overwatch 2 modules, and Siege modules are documented on their own pages. A comparison article should not flatten them into one commercial kit.

If you play CS2 on FACEIT, the useful next URL is the [CS2 product page](/cs2/), not a generic “FACEIT vs matchmaking” checkout. The [homepage directory](/) is the chooser when you are still picking a title.

## How to use this comparison

Use it to decide whether you are reading about the platform or about a game. Platform questions belong on the blog and FAQ. Feature lists and access actions belong on the game route. Status labels belong on the status hub.

If a future guide mentions “regular” versus “FACEIT” for one title, it should still send you to that title’s page instead of treating the comparison as a storefront.
